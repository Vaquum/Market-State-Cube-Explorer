# Market State Cube Explorer

A visualization overlay for independent dyadic time and price grids, using a recorded Binance BTCUSDT spot-trade snapshot.

## Run locally

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8080 --bind 127.0.0.1
```

Open [localhost:8080](http://localhost:8080). The app ships with its snapshot and D3; it makes no external requests and requires no backend, account, or JavaScript build tool. The view lives in the page's address; the workspace, the last view and named views persist in browser local storage.

## Explore

The explorer fills the window. One bar across the top names the market and its state on the left, and holds the controls on the right; the chart takes the rest, the inspector sits on the right and the drawer under the chart. The controls are icons in groups by task: the view (time window, resolution, price axis), Lines, the pointer tools, replay, history and help. Rest the pointer on one for a second, or hold it on a touch screen, and a label names it with its key and says what it does; once one label has shown, the next shows at once. On a phone the window stays in the bar and the rest opens in a sheet, named.

- **Time window** chooses what the chart spans: 15 or 30 minutes, 1, 4, 12 or 24 hours, 7 or 30 days, a year, this year, last year or all history. Each ends at the latest data, except last year, which is the whole calendar year before this one. The number keys 1 to 9 choose them in order and 0 all history; W opens the list. A view no window names shows its span there instead.
- **Resolution** is the button showing the current cell size, such as `15 min × 125`. A padlock shows whether auto level is on (A); in auto, the level follows the cell size on screen. The button opens the **resolution plane**, which maps every level of the lattice as a grid of buttons, marks the diagonal and the current level, and gives each level two separate glyphs that never replace one another: whether it is **ready**, **pending** (the read is under way) or **unavailable** (coarser cells are shown), and the size of its cells (**small** under 6 px on a side, **usable** from 6 to 32 px each way, **large** over 32 px). A key under the grid paints the same tiles. Arrow keys move through the plane, and Enter chooses a level; the steppers beside it are a group of 44 px targets.
- **Price axis** sets how the price range follows a time zoom; its icon shows the mode:
  - **Free** leaves it alone.
  - **Refit** fits it to the visible trades.
  - **Coupled** zooms it by the same factor.
  - **Diagonal** zooms it by the square root of the time factor and keeps the price level on the measured diagonal (D).

  The wheel zooms the axis it's over. On the price labels it zooms the price range alone, around the price under the pointer; on the time labels, time. On the chart it zooms time, and the price range with Shift. What a wheel gesture zooms is fixed at its first tick, by where it starts and whether Shift is held then, until it pauses. Dragging the price labels scales the price range around the price where the drag began: up zooms in, down zooms out. Zooming the price range by hand (the wheel or a drag on the price axis, or Shift with the wheel, = - or a double-click) leaves Refit for Free, so the next refit doesn't undo it.

  Its list also fits the price range once (F).
- **Lines** (P) holds families of lines, each a named group that opens and closes, each row a toggle; any number can be on at once. Each family has one colour (gold for the volume profile, violet for the price levels, olive for the averages and Bollinger bands, rust for the VWAPs, neutral for the clock), and every reference stroke is 1.5 px whatever its timeframe: the timeframe is in each line's tag and name, never in its weight or saturation. How a line is known is its pattern, inside the span it is supported over: **solid** is observed or derived, **dashed** is the held extension to the right edge, and **dotted** is a retrospective lead-in to a confirmation. A line's family and kind are also told by its label and, for the volume profile, an endpoint glyph (a triangle for a POC, a square for the edge of a value area). The bar's button shows a dot for each colour that has lines on (Session levels and Structure are two groups of the one violet family) and a count of the lines on; the footer keys name each family and pattern in use, and Clear turns every line off. Each row has a **Focus** control, and each family one: a focused line is drawn a pixel wider and is never the one the 20% budget leaves out, a chip names it, **Show all** (the menu's, or the chip) ends it, and it is temporary, kept nowhere. The menu says how many of the lines that are on are shown ("11 of 14 references shown") and each row that is not drawn says why: held back by the budget, too close together at this zoom, off screen (its tag stays at the edge), warming up, not read, needing a closer zoom or the live cube, or no event to draw.
  - **Volume profile**, in the POC gold. **POC lines** draw, for each period chosen, a line at the price where it traded the most: the centre of the 125 USDT row with the most USDT volume, the cube's POC at its finest rows. The periods are 1 day, this week, 7 days, this month, 30 days, 90 days, this year, 1 year and 3 years, each up to the latest data, and any days chosen by date, each from that UTC day's start to the latest data. Each line is solid across its period, with a tick where the period starts, and dashed on to the right edge, where a tag names it and gives its price; a line above or below the view keeps its tag at that edge, with an arrow. **Value area high and low** adds the top and bottom of each period's 70% value area. **Daily POC and value area** draws each UTC day's across its day. **Untested daily POCs** (live) are the last 30 sessions' POCs that no later trade has come within 125 USDT of, within 5 daily ATRs of the latest price, each drawn from its day on to the right edge and tagged with its age in sessions and its distance in daily ATRs and USDT; **untested weekly POCs** (live) are the last 26 weeks', the same way.
  - **Session levels** (live), in the levels' violet: each UTC day's open across its day, the previous day's high, low and close across the following day, the weekly open (Monday 00:00 UTC) across its week and the monthly open across its month, all exact from the cube's opens, highs, lows and closes.
  - **Structure** (live), in the same violet: the **all-time high** from its time on; the **prior cycle high**, the highest price before the last fall of more than half that came before the all-time high; **4-hour and daily swings** by the ATR zigzag, where the price reversed from its extreme by more than 3 ATRs (14 bars, Wilder's) of the swing's own timeframe, each shown only once a bar confirms it, in replay too (a reversal seen only on a bar still forming is a candidate, dotted to the edge and named so far in its row and tooltip), marked with a triangle at its extreme, dotted from there to its confirmation and solid from then until a later bar trades through it; two consecutive swing highs or lows of one timeframe within 125 USDT of each other flagged EQH or EQL and joined; and **retracements**, 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% of a move measured back from its later extreme, for the last 30 sessions' range, the last 90 sessions' and the last daily swing. While a timeframe's swings in view would sit closer than 12 px apart, only its unbroken ones draw.
  - **Moving averages** (live), in the averages' olive, each on the closes of its own timeframe and drawn from its first full window at the end of each bar:
    - **Daily:** the 21 EMA and the 50, 100 and 200 SMA, with **golden and death crosses** marked where the 50 SMA crosses above or below the 200 SMA, filled for golden and ringed for death, and each described first as the crossing it is ("50 SMA above 200 SMA").
    - **Weekly:** the **200-week SMA**, which starts in late 2024, 200 weeks into the history, and the **bull market support band**: the 20-week SMA and the 21-week EMA, as two lines.
    - **Intraday, on grid columns:** the 12 and 28 EMA on 3.75-minute bars (standing in for the 5-minute 9 and 21), the 9 and 21 EMA on 15-minute bars, and the 50 and 200 EMA on hourly and on 4-hour bars.
    - **Bollinger bands (20, 2σ)** on 15-minute, hourly, 4-hour and daily bars: the upper, middle and lower bands, each solid and tagged Upper, Middle or Lower. Squeezes, on the 4-hour bars (bandwidth below its 10th percentile of the last 500 bars) and the daily bars (bandwidth at its lowest of the last 182 days), are intervals in the event strip under the price pane, one named lane for each timeframe that is on, marked in one fixed ink and never shaded between the bands.
    - The 15- and 3.75-minute lines draw while their bars are at least a quarter pixel wide, about 42 and 10 days across 1,000 px; wider, the family asks to zoom in. Their bars are read over the view, so while it is before the latest data their rows in the menu give the value at its right edge, and the family says so.
  - **VWAP** (live), in the VWAP rust: the **session VWAP** from each 00:00 UTC across its day, and **anchored VWAPs** from the last daily swing high, from the last daily swing low and from any days chosen by date, each from its anchor on. Each is Σ USDT ÷ Σ BTC, exact from the cube's volumes, drawn at the finest bars at least 2 px wide, and one that reaches the latest data runs on to the right edge at its latest value, tagged.
  - **Clock**, vertical lines in a neutral grey, each kind its own dash: day start, the Monday week open and the month open; funding at 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 UTC; the US equity open, 13:30 UTC in US daylight time and 14:30 otherwise; CME's Friday 16:00 close and Sunday 17:00 reopen in Chicago time, with the spot gap between them outlined and labelled (live) from the reopen until the end of the hourly bar in which a trade reaches the Friday close, and its interval in the event strip; and Deribit's Friday 08:00 UTC expiry, named weekly, monthly (the month's last Friday) or quarterly (a quarter's end) in its tooltip. Every clock line is the same width and each kind has a pattern of its own, shown in the Lines menu. Clock lines run on past the latest data.
  - A per-day, per-week or per-month line, a day's session VWAP, and each clock kind, draws only while its lines are at least 6 px apart; the latest of each keeps its tag. Hovering a line or its tag gives its name, timeframe and price, and more where it has more: a period's volume, an untested POC's age and distance, a swing's confirmation, when it was known and when it was traded through, a retracement's move, an average's value at the bar close nearest the pointer, where it is drawn, and its first full window, a band's upper, middle, lower and bandwidth and whether it is squeezed, a cross's two averages, a VWAP's anchor and the USDT and BTC it sums, a clock line's event and time, the CME gap's size and when it was traded back through. Every annotation built from bars or the calendar also says when it is known (a structural time, not when a read arrived) and whether it is final or so far; `docs/data-and-semantics.md` has the table. The menu shows each line's price while it is on. Lines built on the cube's measures end where its measures end, and the menu says so. In replay everything ends at the replay's edge, so no line uses trades after it.
- **The level line** (X) is one line of your own, in the ink as a long dash-dot ending in a diamond and tagged Level: X places it on the centre of the price row under the pointer, and X again clears it wherever the pointer is. It is kept as a price, so it stays put through zooms and resolution changes, and drawn across the chart and the profile, with a tag at the right edge giving its price, or an arrow tag at the edge while it is off screen. The Lines menu lists it with a button to remove it, and Clear clears it too. On touch, where a long press peeks with the lens, a tap shows the tooltip, whose row section places the line on that row or clears it.
- **Pan, Select, Lens and Inspect** are the pointer tools (V, S, L, E), and each stays chosen until another is. With Pan, a drag moves the view and a click anchors a column. With Select, each drag measures a new rectangle and a click clears it. The selection, and the mark the pointer links to, are drawn in **two tones**, ink with a surface casing, so the boundary stays visible over any fill, over an empty cell and over the replay hatch, and where it coincides with a Geometry outline, a movement stroke, an open cap or the level line. Esc clears the selection, and a second Esc goes back to Pan.
- **Inspect** (E, or the tool beside Lens) is a cursor of your own, read through the same record the tooltip is made from, so a cell, a row, a column, a reference or a lens cell can be read with the keys, a tap or the large steppers and the chooser of its navigator, with no pointer on the chart. The navigator (bottom left of the plot, focused when Inspect starts) has a tab for each surface: **Cells** (←→ the previous and next time cell at the level drawn, ↑↓ the next higher and lower price row), **Rows** (↑↓), **Columns** (←→ the previous and next column or oscillator bar), **References** (↑↓ through every reference that is on, the ones the budget held back too, kept by identity through a redraw: each line and curve, the equal highs and lows, the crosses and the CME gaps, and the calendar kinds, every one under a name of its own, the days of the session VWAP by their date) and **Lens** (the finer record: the lens is held still while the cursor moves in it, and Pin, which stays Pin, promotes it as before). Home and End are the first and last visible item along the surface's axis (the highest and lowest row for Rows). At the edge of what is visible the cursor stays and the edge is announced; the view is never panned (an arrow that means nothing on a surface, or on the navigator's own tabs and buttons, is consumed and does nothing) and a replay edge never moved. Enter or Space opens the record's detail and does nothing else (no anchor, no Pin, no Play); on a reference it also takes the focus, and gives the previous focus back when it closes. Escape closes the detail, then leaves Inspect for the tool it came from, and only a later Escape clears a selection or goes back to Pan. The cursor is an absolute position in time and price: a pan, a zoom or a change of level never erases it, it is remapped to the cell that contains it or says **Outside the view** (Home brings it back), and it is kept nowhere, not in the address and not in a saved view. A tap or click moves it and changes nothing else (not the anchor, the evidence tab, the selection, the replay edge or the history); a long hold peeks with the lens and returns to Inspect. On touch, a tap with several references within a finger's reach shows a chooser with a 44 px row for each rather than guessing. A readout and its legend marker are always visible, a live announcement is made only for a deliberate move, and the Cells table's rows are reached with the arrows, Home and End, with Enter reading the cell in Inspect.
  - **Lens** shows finer cells inside a region while preserving the surrounding view, one to four levels finer (Shift+L); on the live cube they are read from the cube, anywhere in the history. Its depth and **Pin** sit on the chart while it is the tool. Hold Alt, or hold on touch, to inspect temporarily. Enter or **Pin** makes the lens rectangle and resolution the view.
- **Reading the chart:** gridlines and ticks sit on cell edges at every level. The crosshair reads the price and time on the axes. The tooltip gives a cell's span and all four measures (volume, trades, taker-buy volume and taker-buy trades) with its trade size, exact while Shift is held, and over the price profile a row's; with a movement encoding on, its path and dwell too. The row under the pointer is outlined across the chart and the profile, and the tooltip adds the row's USDT and share of the view or selection, and while the underlay shows, its value for the row and the row's relative volume. Each column's amount for the encoding runs in a pane under the prices.
- **Cells** (M; the first menu in the chart header) chooses the encoding that colours the grid by any of the cube's measures: **volume**, **taker flow** (the taker-buy share of the USDT), **delta** (taker-buy minus taker-sell USDT) and **cascade**, how each cell's USDT splits within its parent, the cell one level coarser in time and price that it shares with three others: log₂ of 4 × its share, so 0 is an even quarter and +2 the whole parent, where no other cell in it traded; **trades**, **taker trades** (the taker-buy share of the trades) and **trade size** (USDT per trade); and **geometry**, the occupied cells. Live, the **movement** encodings show how the price moved inside the cells: **path**, how many rows it travelled in each, and **dwell**, each cell's share of its column's time. Cells it moved through or held in without a trade carry the **movement stroke**, a 1.5 px core in the value's colour inset from the cell on a neutral interior with up to 3.5 px of surface backing, so no row colour under it can be mistaken for the movement; those too small to resolve are counted in the footer as **detail unresolved**. The recorded page lists them as live cube only. Every encoding names what it measures. **Amount**, the default for volume, trades and delta, is the observed amount in the cell, so an edge cell or the open column shows its own partial amount; **Intensity**, chosen in the menu's Scale section, divides by the covered time and price (per minute per 125 USDT band) so a cut cell compares with whole ones, and any measure with such a denominator says **Short exposure** when either covered span is under 10% of its nominal one. **Path** is the path over the cell's price span, in row spans, with the USDT moved and the row spans per minute beside it in the tooltip; **dwell** is the cell's share of its column's covered time. Unbounded amounts take a **Value** scale, log1p(|x|/k) over log1p(U/k), where U is the largest and k the median nonzero magnitude of the cells it was fitted on (or a linear one, |x|/U), or **Relative rank**, a 257-knot Type-7 quantile approximation, on one neutral slate ramp from the surface to deep ink; signed measures use a positive and a negative arm from a shared midpoint. Where a signed cell is at least 12 px across each way it also carries a **sign mark** at its centre (a plus above the midpoint, a minus below it, a ring at it; the footer keys them), in the ink or the surface colour that contrasts more with its fill, so the sign is read without the hue; below that size the signed readout, the Cells table and Inspect give it, and the footer says how many cells were left unmarked; the arms are not made to differ in lightness to separate them in grayscale. The taker shares are linear 0–100%, diverging about 50% (a narrower window, symmetric about 50%, can be set in the legend and names its clipping); cascade is log₂ on −2 to +2, ticked at 1/4×, 1/2×, 1×, 2× and 4×. A cell whose parent is still open or only partly loaded has no cascade value, and neither has the coarsest level. A cell without a value is never drawn as a colour or a zero: pending, failed, unsupported and undefined cells get keyed marks, and a cell that traded balanced buys and sells is a zero at the midpoint. Profiles include total and buy POCs and a composite 70% value area; **Column POCs** (Shift+P) draws each column's POC.
- **Columns** (B; beside Cells, and on a phone in the controls sheet) chooses what the pane under the prices shows for each column. **Same as cells**, the default, follows the encoding: each column's amount of it, its signed taker volume or trades under the taker shares and delta, its path over its range under Path, its USDT traded per USDT moved under Dwell, and under Cascade its share of its parent column. Or the pane shows a measure of its own: **volume**, **delta**, **trades**, **trade size**, **efficiency**, each column's USDT per 125 USDT row its trades touched against its parent column's (log₂ of their ratio over the 0.70 expected, so 0 is as expected), and live, **choppiness** (its path over its range) and **volume per path**. Live, it also holds the **oscillators**, each drawn as lines on its own bars along the chart's time axis: **RSI 14 · 1D** and **RSI 14 · 4h**, with 70 and 30 guides and **divergences** marked between consecutive swings of the RSI's own timeframe, as neutral triangles joined by a line and worded as the relationship they are: pointing down for "Price higher, RSI lower" (a higher high with a lower RSI high) and up for "Price lower, RSI higher" (a lower low with a higher RSI low), each once its later swing is confirmed and dotted until then; and **MACD · 1D** (12, 26, 9), its histogram, line and signal, with its crossings marked as triangles (up where MACD crosses above its signal, in the positive role; down where it crosses below, in the negative role) and a named zero. Positive and negative there mean only MACD minus its signal. The pane names its measure and unit at its top left beside its scale, and hovering a column gives its value, or an oscillator's value at the bar close nearest the pointer. The ratios draw from a centre line on the positive and negative roles to ±2, with a marked edge where a value goes beyond it; a column with no defined ratio shows a hollow diamond on the baseline and a measured zero a tick. Every length axis is named: the column axes are **Auto axes** (the exact maximum of the columns shown, frozen while you zoom and updated once you have stopped), RSI is fixed 0–100 with its 30/70 guides, and MACD, its signal and its histogram share one symmetric axis. Efficiency's 0.70 expectation is 2^(ISO_B − 1) of a range-derived model (fitted on levels 6 to 13, history from 2021-01-01, extracted 2026-09-24; not proven neutral at every level): the chip says whether the cutoff lies before that day (**retrospective**), within it (**timing unverified**; the recorded page always does) or after it, and beyond the fitted levels **Model extrapolated beyond fitted levels**. On the recorded page efficiency covers the last 30 days (7 below 15-minute columns), where the snapshot has 125 USDT rows, and choppiness, volume per path and the oscillators are listed as live cube only.
- **Rows** (U; after Columns, and on a phone in the controls sheet) draws an underlay: each price row's value over a **period** of its own, chosen beside it (the POC lines' periods, all history, or since a chosen day; 90 days by default), as a **strip** 12 px wide immediately right of the cells, each row at full strength so that its value can be read, and behind the cells as a **backdrop** at a fixed 16% of that strength, so they show the heavy levels the view never visited; the backdrop is drawn under the cells' fills. The period's profile is the second of the **profile tracks** below, with its POC dashed and its 70% value area marked. **Volume** is its USDT, **delta** its taker-buy minus taker-sell USDT and, live, **time at price** how long the price spent in the row, from the cube's dwell, each the period's amount per row; **relative volume** is log₂ of the row's share of the view's USDT (the selection's, when there is one) over its share of the period's, both taken over the same price support W and the same rows. Identical distributions read 0; a row the period traded and the view didn't sits at the low end with an infinity mark; a row the view traded and the period didn't is marked **no reference**; rows outside W are **outside comparison support**, never zero. At the recorded page's coarse rows only the bins wholly inside W on both sides count, and the legend says so. The colours come from the period's own Explore scale, fitted over all the period's rows including those off screen, and the reference profile's length from its own labelled Auto axis; a legend chip after the menus gives the scale. It is off by default; in replay the period ends at the replay's edge. On the recorded page the last 30 days are summed at 125 USDT rows and longer periods from the snapshot's 1,000 USDT rows, marked ≈; time at price is live cube only.
- **Profile tracks** sit right of the Rows strip, each with its heading and its numeric domain under it. The **current** track is the view's (or the selection's) USDT at each row with its taker-buy part, whatever the Cells encoding; with Rows on, the **reference** track is the Rows measure over its period. **Independent axes** is the default: each track on its own Auto domain, printed. The **Profile** chip beside the legend chips opens the comparison. **Shared absolute** gives both tracks one domain, the same pixels per unit, where they hold the same measure and basis on an exact common price partition (the finer side summed onto the coarser rows; only the bins wholly inside the view are shown, so a bar on one track is never a half bin against a whole one). **Shared row share** draws each row's share of its track's total over the same price window, with both totals shown, for two nonnegative distributions. A choice that cannot be exact is disabled and says why (a signed Delta has no row share, and Shared absolute needs Volume on both tracks). Under 600 px wide both tracks, with the profile's P, B, H and L marks drawn in them, fold behind the chip, which then names both domains and the active measures; **Show the tracks on the chart** brings them back (the Rows strip stays). The data and the enabled state are not lost.
- **Marks that are not data** have fixed shapes in one neutral family, and the footer's keys are painted by the same code. A cell with no trade is a hairline outline on the surface; an **open** column carries a 1.5 px cap along its top edge; a **partial** or coarser cell a short tick across its corner; the archive's edge is a 1 px dashed **provisional** line; and a cell the price moved through is the movement stroke above. Only the POC lines use the gold. Where several of these overlap, they are drawn in one order: the Rows backdrop, the cells, the bounded references and event boundaries, the state marks, the selection, and last the lens and the tooltips.
- **Scales and legends.** Each colour has a legend chip (after the Cells and Rows menus) that opens a dialog with its actual ticks, the value under the pointer on the bar, the measure, basis, unit, transform, mapping id, the cells or rows it was fitted on, how many fall outside its range and the keys of any marks without a value. A scale is **Explore** by default: it is fitted once when the data is complete and settled, then kept for that resolution (or, for Rows, that period and row size), so panning, selecting and live data never recolour the chart; changing resolution selects that context's own scale and says **Scale changed** with the old and new ids. **Comparison lock** holds the mappings and the Auto axes you see across resolutions and periods, so equal amounts keep equal colours and larger coarse amounts may clip (counted); **Auto color** refits after each settled change, at most twice a second; **Fit** replaces a mapping once; **Local contrast** gives the lens its own scale. **Scale range exceeded** appears when over 10% of the marks, or 25% of their area, fall outside a scale's range, and **Low discrimination** when over 90% of the nonzero marks sit in the lowest or highest 5% of the ramp; neither recolours anything. During a replay a scale fitted on data after the replay's edge is never drawn, and live and replay keep separate scales. The Basis, Transform and Scale policy choices are a Scale section in the Cells and Rows menus; no shortcut keys are added.
- **Continuations** compare matching historical states with unconditional outcomes over all price rows, whatever band is on screen. Each outcome opens its cases in the drawer, including opposing outcomes and POC-barrier crossings. **Replay** hides the trades after an anchor and nothing else. Its transport on the chart steps the anchor (`,` and `.`), plays it at ½× to 4× (Space) and returns to now (End).
- **The drawer** holds three tabs:
  - **Cells:** the measures of every occupied cell, sortable and linked to the chart on hover; with a movement view on, each cell's path and dwell, and the cells the price moved through without a trade.
  - **Cases:** the historical cases, each date a jump into replay.
  - **Query:** the six-parameter query, with the measures a movement view shows. Copy queries or portable `origo-cube:` view codes, and restore them in the same app. Below them the **View summary** says, in one place for a screenshot, what the view's colours and lengths mean: the place and level, each channel's measure, basis, unit, transform, scale policy, cohort, fit cutoff, mapping id, support and clipping (the same rows as its Details popover), what the Rows are, each axis's policy and domain, the profile tracks, the data and canonical cutoffs and the replay edge, the model's status, the appearance and its version, and that original vintages are not recorded. It is not an export or a snapshot of the data; **Copy summary** copies its text.
- **The address holds the view**: its window or rectangle, the level when it is locked, the encoding, the pane's measure, the underlay and its period, overlays, every line on with its days, the level line and replay. It always starts `vis=2` (visual version 2) and names the appearance (`ap=`), and carries the active scales and frozen axes (`sc=`). An address over 8,192 characters drops to scale ids only, then to settings only ("settings-only URL, not exact calibration"), and **Copy view code** always holds the complete portable view (at most 1 MiB decoded, at most 16 scales, 257 rank knots each). An older address or saved view opens with its settings kept and its old hidden rate/rank colours replaced by Amount, Value and Explore, with one notice naming the change; nothing saved is overwritten until you save again. Copy it to share or bookmark a view; each tab keeps its own. A window is relative and opens on the latest data (last year on the year before the latest data's); any other view opens where it was. A scale a link or a code carries was fitted at a level; a window refits its prices when it opens, so the page can settle at another level, and then it fits its own scale and says so, naming both levels, instead of drawing another colour silently.
- **History:** every move is an entry in the browser's history, so Back and Forward (the browser's, ⌘[ and ⌘] or Alt+← and Alt+→, or the bar's arrows) walk it, and steps of one kind in quick succession count as one. Back returns to a place and leaves the encoding and overlays as they are. The history list names each entry's range and level, and survives a reload.
- **Views** (H) keeps named views in this browser for every tab: name the current view and save it (Shift+S). A view saved while it shows the latest data opens on the latest data, with the same span and the price range fitted again; any other opens where it was.
- **Layout:** the inspector and the drawer resize and collapse, and the page remembers how you left them.

Text on the chart stands on an opaque plate, so it is read against a known colour and never against a cell: tags and labels are the ink on the surface, with a bar of the line's hue. On touch every control is a 44 px target each way (the resolution plane's compact buttons and the canvas's marks are dense visuals, reached through the steppers, the plane's keys and the Inspect navigator), and a change of the system's reduced-motion setting takes effect at once, on the canvas and in the page.

**Candles:** choose Candles in the leftmost menu, or press **K** to toggle between Candles and the previous encoding (initially Volume). It replaces price cells with exact OHLC bodies and wicks: hollow blue up, filled clay down, neutral unchanged. Prices never snap to rows. The lens shows finer candles; hover, Inspect and the table show exact prices and measured coverage. Candles requires the live cube; the recorded snapshot shows explicit unavailability.

Keys work anywhere on the page except in text fields, and every control's tooltip names its key; `?` lists them all. On a phone the chart comes first and the controls open in a sheet.

## Live cube data

On the Origo host the explorer runs beside the market state cube ([PRD-0022](https://github.com/Vaquum/Origo/issues/462)) and reads it live. The cube's query service answers with paths to Arrow files on its own volume, so `tools/cube_bridge.py` runs where that volume is mounted: it asks the service for cells, reads the files through the cube's supported reader (`tools/market_state_reader.py`, a pinned copy from Origo 3.28.0) and serves `index.html` with a live pack in place of the recorded snapshot, behind the Vaquum portal login.

The live pack holds the same three tiers as the snapshot: the last seven days at base resolution, 30 completed days at 15 minutes, and the whole history at 64 hours × 1,000 USDT. They are read up to the cube's own data cutoff, so the latest trades are in them: the base column that holds the cutoff is the **open** one, drawn and counted as unfinished. The partitions the tiers share are checked to carry the same revision and build id (the pack is read again if the cube changed underneath), and the pack token digests every pin it read. A partition's generation is not compared: attaching a new component to a build, as the cube's history upgrade does, moves it without changing any cell. It is rebuilt at most once a minute. Where the cube's archived days end, the chart marks where **provisional** minutes begin, which the day's archive may still revise; the top bar's state says the same.

Every number is the cube's answer for the rectangle it describes. The selection, or the view when there is none, is rounded to base edges as the cube rounds them, and its four totals, both POCs and its cells come from the loaded cells when they tile it exactly (the last seven days, for any rectangle), and otherwise from the cube itself: `GET /cube/query?n&m&b0&b1&r0&r1&pack`. Until the cube answers, the inspector says it is measuring and shows no number. The open column always comes from the page's own pack, so a cube that has moved on since never mixes into an answer. The same route reads a tile when no loaded block can draw the view at its level (at most 4,096 columns; a wider view is drawn with coarser columns), the lens's finer cells, each POC line, and at 8-hour columns the days whose profiles the daily and untested POCs need that no loaded tier holds; `GET /cube/columns?n&m&pack` reads the history the continuations compare, each column's POC and volumes for up to the last 100,000 columns at the drawn level. Reads go one at a time, most needed first. A read is refused unless every partition it read is one the page's pack read, at the same revision; the page then takes the cube's new data first and asks again, rather than mixing two cube states. Nothing is substituted when a read fails, and the page says why.

**Efficiency** counts the 125 USDT rows each column's trades touched from the loaded tiers where they hold a column's parent whole, and elsewhere asks `GET /cube/touched?n&b0&b1&pack`: each column's USDT and rows touched at the pane's level and one level up, which the bridge computes from a `/cube/query` at 125 USDT rows and answers as counts and sums only, 512 columns at a time. It is read only while Efficiency shows, after the view's own reads, and kept per pack and span.

**The underlay's rows** are the POC lines' per-period rows, every 125 USDT row of them kept: summed from loaded tiers that tile the period, split at the recent tier's first UTC midnight for periods with a fixed start, or read with `/cube/query` at m = 0 over at most 16 columns, once per period and span, only while the underlay shows and after the view's own reads. Time at price reads the period's dwell the same way with `motion=1`, in path and dwell's second slot, up to the pack's last complete base column.

**Bars** (`GET /cube/bars?n&b0&b1&pack`) are read while Candles or a line built on them is on, in the same second slot and after the view's own reads: at dyadic timeframes n = 0..20, each column's open, high, low and close, its USDT and BTC volume and its trades, from the cube's own measures at rows that hold every price. Candles requests the visible span plus one interval each side, before indicator backfill, with at most 4,096 bars and bounded numerical caching. A fractional `b1` preserves an exact replay edge; the forming live base interval is excluded. The session levels and untested POCs read the 8-hour bars and the CME gap the hourly ones, over the whole history in chunks of 4,096 bars, the latest first; the chunk holding the latest data is read again from its latest bar as the data advances.

**Path and dwell** ([PRD-0023](https://github.com/Vaquum/Origo/issues/478)) are read only while a view shows them, in the cube service's second query slot and never ahead of the view's own reads, so they never hold a view back: `GET /cube/motion?tier&pack` reads the tier the view is drawn from with them, whole once and afterwards only the columns it gained (`&from`), and `/cube/tile` and `/cube/query` with `motion=1` read the view's or the lens's tile, and the rectangle's where no loaded block tiles it. They end at the pack's last complete base column, so the open column is measured once it completes; the inspector says where they end.

An open page stays live. Just after each pack can be rebuilt, it asks `GET /cube/pack?since=<its pack token>` and puts the answer in place: nothing when it holds the current pack; the columns each tier gained when its pack is a prefix of the current one, every partition it read unchanged and every column it keeps the same (the same trade counts, and volumes to within the last bits the cube's float sums may round differently between two reads); otherwise, after a revision or a backfill, the whole pack. A view that shows the cutoff follows it as data arrives, and a window keeps its length; any other view stays where it is, and **Latest** (End) brings it back. The top bar reads **LIVE** with how long ago the data last advanced, and says plainly when the cube has no new data or the server stops answering. The tab's title carries the latest POC. A page open across a deploy offers to reload.

The committed `index.html` keeps the recorded snapshot, so the page also works from a plain static server and reads **RECORDED** there.

## Recorded data

The snapshot cutoff is **2026-09-24 12:02:48.750 UTC**. This is a recorded prototype, not a live feed.

| Loaded history | Finest time resolution | Finest price resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Last seven days | 56.25 seconds | 125 USDT |
| Last 30 completed days | 15 minutes | 125 USDT |
| History from 2021-01-01 UTC | 64 hours | 1,000 USDT |

Source blocks overlap; historical cases are deduplicated. Finer historical detail is unavailable where the snapshot doesn't hold it: there, a rectangle's numbers count only the snapshot's whole cells inside it, marked **Ready part**, and POC lines and their value areas use the finest rows that cover their period, marked ≈; daily POCs are drawn for the last 30 days only. The session levels, the untested POCs, the CME gap, structure, the moving averages, Bollinger bands, the oscillators and the VWAPs need the cube's exact prices and volumes and are live cube only; the clock lines are drawn here too. The live cube measures both exactly. Zero-trade, unfinished and unavailable cells have distinct states.

Empirical continuation shares are descriptive, not calibrated forecasts. Samples overlap. Matching percentages and boxes are withheld below 30 cases. Barriers measure **first column-end POC crossings**, not trade-level first-touch events. See [data and semantics](docs/data-and-semantics.md).

## Deploy

The explorer runs on the Origo host, `37.27.112.167`, as the Compose project `cube-explorer` in `/opt/cube-explorer`:

- `explorer` builds the `Dockerfile` (Python 3.12, pyarrow, numpy), mounts the cube's result volume `tdw-control-plane_market-state` read-only at `/opt/origo/market-state`, uses the host network so the cube service is `127.0.0.1:8486`, and serves the page on `127.0.0.1:8487` only, as a non-root user on a read-only filesystem. It checks no credentials of its own.
- TLS and the login are the host's Caddy ingress on port 443, deployed from [Vaquum/Portal](https://github.com/Vaquum/Portal) (`/opt/portal/caddy/Caddyfile`): its `cube.vaquum.fi` site passes a request on to `127.0.0.1:8487` only after the Vaquum portal login, over a Let's Encrypt certificate. `cube.vaquum.fi` is a DNS-only A record for the host.

Every push to `main` deploys through `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`, which first runs the checks of `.github/workflows/check.yml` and continues only if they passed for that exact commit; then it syncs the checkout to the host with rsync, runs `docker compose up -d --build`, and checks over SSH that the explorer answers 200. It needs the secret `DEPLOY_SSH_KEY` and the variables `DEPLOY_HOST`, `DEPLOY_USER`, `DEPLOY_DIR` and `DEPLOY_KNOWN_HOSTS`.

Local check of the server, which needs the cube volume and service and therefore runs on the host:

```sh
python3 tools/cube_bridge.py --port 8487
```
