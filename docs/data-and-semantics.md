# Data and semantics

## Cube coordinates

Time starts at 2021-01-01 00:00:00 UTC. The base cell is 56.25 seconds × 125 USDT. Time and price exponents are independent nonnegative integers. Time edges are anchored to the history start; price edges are multiples of the price resolution. Intervals include the lower edge and exclude the upper edge.

The copyable query has six parameters: `t1`, `t2`, `p1`, `p2`, `tR`, `pR`. Requested bounds snap to base-cell edges with exact midpoints rounding upward, and the end of a rectangle that reaches the latest data is the edge that closes the open base column. Selecting a rectangle preserves those bounds as resolution changes. On the live cube the numbers are the cube's answer for exactly these bounds, edge cells included: summed from loaded cells when they tile the rectangle, and otherwise read from the cube. The time level can be coarser than `tR` when the rectangle spans more than 4,096 columns; totals and POCs don't depend on it. In the recorded snapshot, where no block tiles a rectangle, the numbers cover the snapshot's whole cells inside it, and the UI names that part; it does not invent finer cells.

## Measures

Each occupied cell carries USDT volume, trade count, taker-buy USDT volume and taker-buy count. Taker buys mean `is_buyer_maker = 0`. A POC is the center of the price row with the greatest summed volume; ties choose the lower row. The buy POC uses taker-buy volume. Rows without qualifying volume have no POC.

Signed taker volume is `2 × buy_volume − volume`. Volume, trades and delta are **observed amounts**: a cell's own, partial where the rectangle's edge or the open column cuts it. The explicit **Intensity** basis is the amount × 60 × 125 ÷ (covered seconds × covered price width in USDT), in a unit per minute per 125 USDT price band, so a partial cell compares with a whole one; a positive exposure is never floored, a zero or unknown one is a non-value, and a measure whose covered time or price is under 10% of its nominal span is labelled **Short exposure** (a usability cue, not confidence). Trade size is volume per trade and is undefined for a cell with no trades. Taker flow and taker trades are the taker-buy share of the USDT or of the trades, linear 0–100% with 50% as the midpoint; delta is signed and a cell whose buys equal its sells is a zero, not a cell without trades. Unbounded measures are coloured by a fitted scale (see Scales and calibration). The contiguous 70% value area expands from the POC into adjacent rows, choosing the larger adjacent volume and the lower row on ties.

Live, a cell also has how the price moved inside it ([PRD-0023](https://github.com/Vaquum/Origo/issues/478)): its **path length**, the USDT the price travelled between consecutive trades inside the cell, each move belonging to the later trade's column and split across the rows it passes; and its **dwell**, the seconds the price spent in the cell's rows, each trade's price holding until the next trade. A column's dwell over all its rows is its time. A cell the price moved through or held in without a trade has path or dwell but no trades: such cells count for path and dwell only, and geometry, the cell counts and every other measure keep meaning the cells with trades. **Path** is a cell's path over its price span, in row spans (the drawn row size), with no extrapolation of the covered time; its USDT moved, and 60 × path ÷ (width × seconds) in row spans per minute, are read beside it. **Dwell** is the covered seconds in the cell over the column's covered seconds in the rectangle, a linear 0–100% share that is not renormalised: the residual time no row accounts for stays visible where the column's total over all rows is known and reads "not measurable" otherwise, because the protocol reports only where a measurement ends, not interior gaps. A dwell that is materially negative or exceeds the covered time is a failed input and is never clamped. The cells without a trade are outlined. The pane under the prices shows, under Path, each column's path over its range (its highest trade less its lowest: 1 for a straight run, more the more it turned back), and under Dwell, its USDT volume over its path. Path and dwell are read up to the pack's last complete base column, so the open column is measured once it completes. The recorded snapshot has neither.

**Cascade** shades a cell by its share of its parent, the cell one level coarser in both time and price, (n + 1, m + 1), that it shares with three others: log₂(4v ÷ V) for the cell's volume v and its parent's V, both summed from the drawn block's own cells, so the shares within a parent add up to 1. The share is the whole cell's even where the rectangle's edge cuts through the cell; its tooltip then gives the whole cell's USDT and its parent's too. 0 is an even quarter, +1 twice that and −1 half; +2 is the whole parent, where no other cell in it traded, so the price never got there: that is movement, not concentration. The value is a log₂ coordinate on −2 to +2, ticked at 1/4×, 1/2×, 1×, 2× and 4×, on the positive arm above 0 and the negative arm below from the shared midpoint; a value beyond the ends is clipped for drawing only and counted, and the column version, log₂(2v ÷ V) with a natural maximum of +1, uses the same axis. A parent that starts before the block's cells or ends after them has no value; one that runs past the data (the open column, or a replay's edge) is unfinished; and at the lattice's coarsest time (n = 20) or price (m = 9) there is no parent.

The pane under the prices shows one value per column. Its measure is the Columns menu's: **Same as cells** follows the encoding as described above, and under Cascade shows each column's share of its parent column one level up in time, as log₂(2 × share); or the pane shows volume, delta, trades, trade size, **choppiness** (path ÷ (high − low), as under Path), **volume per path** (USDT ÷ path, as under Dwell) or **Efficiency**. For a column c and its parent column p one level up in time, Efficiency is log₂((E_c ÷ E_p) ÷ 0.70), where E is the column's USDT volume over the number of 125 USDT rows its trades touched, counted at 125 USDT whatever rows are drawn: at coarse rows a column spans one or two rows, and the measure would collapse to plain volume. 0.70 is the expected ratio: halving a column halves its volume while its range shrinks by 2^0.486, the diagonal's exponent (see below), and 2^(0.486 − 1) = 0.700. A parent's rows are those its two columns touched, each counted once. The rows are counted from loaded blocks of 125 USDT rows where one holds a column's parent whole (the recent tier's base cells, and from 15 minutes the 30-day archive with the recent tier after it), so the recorded snapshot has Efficiency for its last 30 days (7 below 15-minute columns), and live from the cube elsewhere. The ratios, log₂ of the actual over the expected, draw from a centre line on the positive and negative colours, full at ±2; like Cascade's, a column whose parent runs past the data is unfinished. They describe whole columns, while the pane's other measures describe the rectangle's cells.

A POC line is the POC of one period at the cube's finest rows, 125 USDT: the rolling periods end at the edge that closes the data (1 day, 7, 30 and 90 days, 1 and 3 years of 365 days), the calendar ones start on this UTC week's Monday, month or year, and a chosen day starts its period at that UTC day's start. In replay each ends at the replay's edge instead. Live, a line is summed from loaded base cells where they cover its period and read from the cube otherwise; a period with a fixed start (a chosen day, this week, month or year) that begins before those cells is read from the cube up to their first UTC midnight, once a day, and summed from them after it, which gives the same POC; in the recorded snapshot a period its base cells don't cover uses the finest rows that do, and is marked approximate.

A period's **value area** is its 70% value area at the same rows, grown from its POC as the profile's is: each step adds the neighbouring row with more USDT, the lower one on a tie, until the area holds 70% of the period's USDT. Its **high** is the top edge of its highest row and its **low** the bottom edge of its lowest.

**Visible range** in Lines is a POC period whose endpoints follow the visible time interval, snapped to the nearest base edges and bounded by the data or replay edge. It sums across all prices at 125 USDT rows; price panning, price resolution and selection do not redefine it. The value-area toggle adds this period's high and low. **Visible range VWAP** is a horizontal level, Σ USDT ÷ Σ BTC over the same time interval, excluding the forming base interval as bars do. Its read clips both boundary bars to the requested interval, so neither off-screen time nor a coarse bar's outside trades contribute. These references update after time pan or zoom and persist as choices in links, view codes and saved Views; while a new range is pending, no prior range's value stands in. Recorded POCs use the finest available rows and carry the existing approximation label; VWAP requires the live cube.

**Bars** are read from the cube's measures (PRD-0023) at a grid timeframe: 3.75 minutes (n = 2), 15 minutes (n = 4), 1 hour (n = 6), 4 hours (n = 8) and 8 hours (n = 9), each aligned to UTC by the lattice's start. A bar's **open** and **close** are its column's first and last trade by trade ID, its **high** and **low** its highest and lowest trade, its **volume** the USDT traded and its **BTC volume** the base quantity, read at rows of 125 × 2²⁰ USDT, so that one row holds every price and each column is one cell. A **UTC day** is its three 8-hour bars combined (the first one's open, the highest high, the lowest low, the last one's close, the volumes summed), a **week** its seven days from Monday 00:00 UTC, and a **month** its days from the 1st. Bars end at the pack's last complete base column, or where the cube's measures end while it still measures its history; in replay the bar at the edge is read up to the edge.

The **daily ATR** is Wilder's average true range over 14 days, from the history's first day: a day's true range is its high less its low, or further to the previous day's close where that lies outside them; the first ATR is the mean of the first 14 days' true ranges, and each day after it (13 × the last ATR + the day's true range) ÷ 14.

A **daily POC** is the POC of one UTC day at 125 USDT rows, and its value area grows as a period's does; a **weekly POC** is its seven days' rows summed. Each day's profile is summed from loaded cells at 125 USDT rows that tile it (the last 30 days) or read from the cube at 8-hour columns. An **untested POC** is a whole day's POC, within the last 30 sessions, or a whole week's, within the last 26 weeks, that no later trade has come within one 125 USDT row of: no 8-hour bar after its day or week has a low to high range that meets the POC ± 125 USDT. Only those within 5 daily ATRs of the latest price (the last bar's close) are drawn, each with its **age** in sessions or weeks and its distance from the latest price in daily ATRs and in USDT. In replay the days, bars and ATR end at the replay's edge.

The **session levels** are each UTC day's open, drawn across that day; the previous day's high, low and close, drawn across the day after it; each week's open, Monday 00:00 UTC, across its week; and each month's open across its month.

**Structure** is built on the days and on the 4-hour bars, up to the data's edge. The **all-time high** is the highest trade in the history, drawn from the start of the 8-hour bar that holds it. The **prior cycle high** is the highest price before the last **deep low** that came before the all-time high, where a deep low is a day whose low is under half the highest price before it (on Binance's history, November 2021's 69,000 USDT). **Swings** come from the ATR zigzag, on the days with the daily ATR and on the 4-hour bars with Wilder's 14-bar ATR of the 4-hour bars: it follows the running extreme since the last swing, and once a bar reverses from it by more than 3 × the ATR as of the bar before, the extreme is a swing, timestamped at the extreme (a daily swing at the 8-hour bar holding it) and confirmed at the end of the bar that reversed. A bar that makes a new extreme doesn't also confirm a reversal from it; before the first swing, the first reversal from either extreme sets the direction, the high's on a tie; the zigzag starts at the first bar with an ATR before it. A swing is **traded through** at the end of the first later bar whose high is above a swing high, or whose low is under a swing low. Two consecutive swing highs, or lows, of one timeframe within 125 USDT of each other are **equal** (EQH, EQL). A **retracement** measures 0.382, 0.5 and 0.618 of a move back from its later extreme, `later − f × (later − earlier)`: the move between the highest high and the lowest low of the last 30 or 90 sessions, the current one included, or between the last two daily swings. Each extreme is timed at its 8-hour bar, and where both fall in the same 8-hour bar, the bar's own direction, its close against its open, orders them. In replay nothing after the edge is read, so a swing appears only once the trades before the edge confirm it. A reversal seen only on a bar that is still forming (live, the latest bar so far; in replay, the bar at the edge, up to it) makes a **candidate**, not a confirmed swing: its row says so far, and it is confirmed, with that bar's end as its known-at, only when the bar ends with the reversal still in it.

A **VWAP** is Σ USDT ÷ Σ BTC over its span, from the bars' volume and BTC volume, which the cube sums exactly; the page adds them with Neumaier's compensated summation. The **session VWAP** runs from each 00:00 UTC to the day's end; an **anchored VWAP** runs from its anchor to the latest data: the start of the 8-hour bar that holds the last daily swing high, or low, or a chosen UTC day's 00:00. A curve is drawn at the end of each bar of the finest grid timeframe whose bars are at least 2 px wide on screen (3.75 minutes to 8 hours); the part of an anchored VWAP before the bars read for the view is summed from 8-hour bars, so every point is its anchor's whole sum to then. Each day's session VWAP is drawn while days are at least 6 px apart on screen, and otherwise only the last day's in view. In replay each ends at the replay's edge, the bar at the edge read up to the edge.

**Moving averages, Bollinger bands and the oscillators** each run on the closes of their own timeframe. The days and weeks come from the 8-hour bars; the history's first week, which starts on a Friday, is left out, so every weekly window is whole weeks. The 4-hour and hourly bars are read over the whole history. The 15- and 3.75-minute bars are read over the chunks of 4,096 bars the view is in and the chunk before, and computed from that chunk's start: after 4,096 bars an EMA's seed weighs in by under 10⁻¹⁸, so each value is as it would be from the history's start. Those two draw only while their bars are at least a quarter pixel wide, and while the view is before the latest data the menu gives their value at its right edge rather than the latest. Each value is drawn at the end of its bar, whose close it takes in, and from its first full window on; in replay the bars end at the replay's edge, the bar at the edge read up to it. The definitions:

- **SMA(n):** the mean of the last n closes.
- **EMA(n):** α = 2 ÷ (n + 1), seeded with the SMA of its first n closes: from then on, α × the close + (1 − α) × the EMA before.
- **Golden and death crosses:** where SMA(50) − SMA(200) of the daily closes changes sign, golden to above and death to below. A difference of exactly 0 carries the sign before it, so a touch that turns back is no cross.
- **Bull market support band:** the weekly SMA(20) and EMA(21), drawn as two lines.
- **Bollinger (20, 2σ):** SMA(20) ± 2 population standard deviations of the last 20 closes; the **bandwidth** is (upper − lower) ÷ middle.
- **Squeezes:** on the 4-hour bars, bandwidth below its 10th percentile over the last 500 bars, this one included, the percentile taken 0.9 of the way from the 50th lowest of them to the 51st (0.1 × 499 = 49.9 between order statistics numbered from 0); on the daily bars, bandwidth at its lowest of the trailing 182 days, this one included. A run of squeezed bars is one interval in the event strip under the price pane, from the start of its first bar to the end of its last; each bar qualifies at its own close, and the run is final once a later complete bar does not qualify (until then, so far). Nothing is filled between the bands.
- **RSI(14):** the first average gain and loss are the means of the first 14 changes; then each is (13 × the last + this change's gain or loss) ÷ 14, Wilder's smoothing, and RSI = 100 − 100 ÷ (1 + gain ÷ loss), 100 with no loss and 0 with no gain.
- **RSI divergence:** between consecutive swing highs, or lows, of the RSI's timeframe (S5's daily and 4-hour swings), each at its swing's bar: bearish where price made a higher high and the RSI a lower high, bullish where price made a lower low and the RSI a higher low, known once the later swing is confirmed; while that swing is a candidate on a bar still forming, the divergence is dotted (the lead-in pattern) and named so far. It is drawn in neutral ink with a triangle at each swing, pointing down for the higher high and up for the lower low, and worded "Price higher, RSI lower" or "Price lower, RSI higher": a relationship, not a sign.
- **MACD:** EMA(12) − EMA(26) of the daily closes from the 26th day, its **signal** the EMA(9) of MACD seeded with the mean of its first nine values, its **histogram** MACD − signal; a **cross** is where MACD − signal changes sign, as above.

The **clock** lines are times on the calendar: each day's start (00:00 UTC), each week's (Monday 00:00 UTC) and month's (the 1st, 00:00 UTC); funding at 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 UTC; the US equity open, 9:30 in New York, which is 13:30 UTC in US daylight time (from the second Sunday in March to the first Sunday in November) and 14:30 otherwise, Monday to Friday; CME's close, Friday 16:00 in Chicago, and its reopen, Sunday 17:00 in Chicago, each converted with the daylight time of its date; and Deribit's expiry, Fridays at 08:00 UTC, monthly on the month's last Friday and quarterly on the last Friday of March, June, September and December. The **CME gap** spans the spot prices at a weekend's close and reopen, each the last trade before it (the close of the hour ending there); it is outlined and labelled from the reopen until a later hourly bar reaches the Friday close, and it is **filled**, and known as filled, at the end of that hour; while that bar forms the gap reads open and its tooltip says so far. A per-day, per-week or per-month line and each clock kind is drawn only while its lines are at least 6 px apart on screen; the latest of each still draws its tag.

**Event times and known-at.** Every annotation drawn from source bars or from the calendar has an event location and a known-at, published as one table in the module (`E.readout.events.TABLE`) and shown as *Known at*, *Status* and *Source* in its tooltip. Known-at is the structural time of the calculation, never the time a read arrived, and no indicator's arithmetic is bent to make it cleaner.

| Annotation | Event location | Known-at |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed swing | the extreme's supported bar or time | the end of the bar that confirmed the reversal; the lead-in to it is retrospective |
| Equal swing pair | the two extremes | the later swing's confirmation |
| RSI divergence | the compared swings' locations | the later swing's confirmation, not when the RSI's extrema occurred |
| Moving-average or MACD crossing | the crossing bar's end | that complete bar's end; on a bar still forming it is a candidate, so far, not confirmed |
| Bollinger squeeze | the interval of the qualifying bars | each bar's own close, final once a later complete bar does not qualify |
| CME spot gap | the reopen and the spot prices at the boundaries | the gap at the reopen, from the closes available then; its fill no earlier than the end of the hourly bar that crossed |
| Period POC and value area | the stated period's span | a retrospective summary as of its measurement cutoff, not when the period started |
| Untested level | the original POC's period | a status as of the current or replay edge; a later test does not rewrite an earlier replay status |
| Historical continuation range | the anchor and the horizon | the anchor, from the cases that had ended by it; below 30 cases the percentages and boxes are withheld |
| Clock | the scheduled calendar time | a calendar definition, not a measured trade event |

A **candidate** exists only on a bar that is still forming at the data's edge. It has no known-at, reads so far and names the bar it waits for, and it is a confirmed record only once that bar is complete. The readouts of a cell, a column and a Rows band carry the same idea in their `when` block: a complete cell is known at its end, an open or cut one has no known-at yet and says why, and a Rows band is known as of the cutoff its period was measured at.

The **underlay** (Rows) draws each price row's value over a period of its own: the POC lines' periods, all history, or from a chosen UTC day, each ending where the lines' periods end, at the data's edge or a replay's. Its rows are those same per-period rows, every one kept rather than only the top one, summed to the view's row size so that one band lines up with each row of cells. **Volume** is a row's USDT, **delta** its taker-buy minus taker-sell USDT, and **time at price** its dwell over the period, the seconds the price spent in it, read with path and dwell up to the pack's last complete base column. **Relative volume** (version 2) is log₂((current_r ÷ Σ_W current) ÷ (period_r ÷ Σ_W period)) over one declared price support W, the selection's or the view's, and one row partition, summed from the same covered rows on both sides: identical conditional distributions give 0 whatever W is, and +1 is twice the period's share. A row the period traded but the rectangle didn't is negative-infinite (drawn at the low end with an infinity mark); a row the rectangle traded but the period didn't has **no reference**; a row outside W is **outside comparison support**, never a zero. Where the recorded page's rows are coarser than the view's, only the bins wholly inside W on both sides count and the legend names the restriction; if none does the result is unsupported. The strip (12 px, immediately right of the cells) and the backdrop behind the cells (the same entries at a fixed 16%) take their colours from the period's own Explore scale, fitted over every measured row of the period including off-screen rows, and the reference profile's length is a separately labelled axis (see Profile tracks); the period's POC is its row with the most USDT, the lower row winning a tie, and its 70% value area grows from the POC as the profile's does. In the recorded snapshot periods within the last 30 days are summed at 125 USDT rows, one reaching past the recent tier's 7 days from the 30-day archive's first whole 15-minute column after it starts, which the page names; longer periods use the snapshot's 1,000 USDT rows and 64-hour columns, marked ≈, and time at price is live only.

**Profile tracks** are two adjacent tracks right of the Rows strip, drawn on the price rows of the chart. The current track is the view's, or the selection's, USDT at each row with its taker-buy part, whatever the Cells encoding; the reference track, present while Rows is on, is the Rows measure over its period. **Independent axes** is the default and adds nothing: each track has its own registered Auto axis (the exact maximum of the rows it shows; a no-data or zero-only axis is named, never a maximum of 1), printed under it. **Shared absolute** is offered only for Volume against Volume and an exact common price partition: the finer side is coarsened by exact summation onto the coarser row size (a coarse row is never split) and both tracks take one domain, so the same length is the same amount; only the bins wholly inside the view are shown, so a bin that straddles the view's edge is never a half bin on one side against a whole bin on the other. **Shared row share** is offered for two nonnegative distributions (Volume or Time at price on the reference side): each track draws each row's share of its own total over one window W, the rows wholly inside both the view and the reference's support, and both totals are shown; a total of zero is undefined, never a share of 0. A signed Delta and a ratio are not distributions. A mode that is not offered is disabled with its reason (the view or the period is not measured yet, there is no reference, the row sizes have no exact partition, unlike measures, a signed measure, no common window, a zero total), and the plan falls back to independent without discarding the choice. On a chart under 600 px wide both tracks, with the profile's P, B, H and L marks drawn in them, are folded behind the chip unless opened; the chip names the active measures and both domains, and the Rows strip stays. The choice is `pc` in the address (`a` for absolute, `s` for share, left out for independent) and the fold `po` (`1` open, left out for closed); both travel in a view code.

Counts in this snapshot are exact within JavaScript's safe integer range. The full-history trade count is 6,173,120,060. Volumes use Float64 arithmetic and retain normal floating-point summation differences.

## Typed results

A measure that has no value says why; nothing is drawn as a number it does not have. The results are: finite, **negative-infinite** (positive totals, current share 0, reference share above 0), **no-reference** (current above 0, reference 0), empty-both, empty-population (a required total is 0), undefined (a named denominator is 0), no-coarser-parent, waiting-for-complete-parent (an open or partly loaded parent), outside-support, hidden (beyond a replay's edge), pending, failed, unsupported and invalid-input (a dwell that is materially negative or over the covered time). A read that is pending, failed or unsupported is reported as that, before any arithmetic. Only the numerical result tags and counts are stored; a result is never a raw JSON `NaN` or `Infinity`. Zero Volume or Trades may be the same fact as no trades; zero Delta does not mean no trades, and an unavailable value is never a zero.

## Scales and calibration

A colour is a function of a measurement under a **scale**, and every scale has an identity. The identity is a 96-bit hash of the transform, its parameters or knots, and its clip policy; it excludes the measure, units, context, appearance and theme. The measurement is the physical quantity supplied to the scale, the scale coordinate its transformed position, and neither is a colour; the tooltip, the table, the legend marker and the encoder read one record, so their numbers agree.

- **Fixed scales** need no fit: shares are linear 0–100% (the taker shares diverge about 50%), log₂ ratios run −2 to +2, RSI is 0–100. A narrower share window must be symmetric about 50% for the taker shares, persists, and names its clipping.
- **Value** fits U (the largest finite magnitude) and k (the median finite nonzero magnitude, pooled over both signs for a signed measure) and maps log1p(|x|/k) ÷ log1p(U/k); the linear alternative is |x| ÷ U. No finite value in the fitted cohort exceeds 1. An empty cohort is **No calibration** (never U = 1); an all-zero cohort is a **zero-only** calibration and a later nonzero value is out of domain until fitted; an all-equal nonzero cohort maps to the maximum.
- **Relative rank** is a declared 257-knot Type-7 quantile approximation (q = j/256 over the positive finite observations), interpolating between distinct values and mapping a repeated value to the midpoint of its first and last q; it is not an exact empirical midrank.
- **Contexts.** Cells: instrument, measure and basis, formula, transform, the effective level (n and m, not the requested ones) and the live or replay workspace. Rows: the period's identity (rolling duration, the calendar period's resolved start, the since-day anchor or the history start), the effective row size and the quality (exact, approximate rows, approximate start), and not n. The advancing end of a period is provenance, not identity. Cells and Rows never share a scale because their units match.
- **Policies.** **Explore** fits a context once from fully measured cells in the view or selection (or all measured rows of a Rows period) when the data is complete at one generation and no gesture has run for 200 ms; it then holds until Fit or a different context. **Comparison lock** holds the mappings and Auto axes you see; **Auto color** refits after each settled change, at most every 500 ms; **Local contrast** gives the lens its own descriptor. Up to 64 contexts are kept per workspace; the active and locked ones are never evicted, and a re-initialisation after eviction says so.
- **Axes.** Column and profile lengths are registered Auto axes: the exact maximum of the displayed values (a zero-only axis and a no-data axis are named, never a maximum of 1), frozen during a gesture and while a replay plays, and updated on the settled schedule.
- **Warnings.** **Scale range exceeded** shows when over 10% of the occupied marks or 25% of their screen area lie outside the range; **Low discrimination** when over 90% of the nonzero marks lie in the lowest or the highest 5% of the ramp. They recolour nothing.
- **Replay.** A scale fitted on data later than the replay's effective cutoff is invalidated before it is drawn; live and replay keep separate scales and leaving a replay restores the live one. An explicit Comparison lock taken live stays in an earlier replay, labelled as an external comparison override.

## Model and observation provenance

Observation provenance (the source, the cutoff, the canonical-through boundary and the opaque pack token), the empirical model's provenance and the scale's calibration provenance are recorded separately: eligible data and an eligible scale do not make a later-estimated model contemporaneous. The cutoff, the boundary and the token are equality facts, **not a reconstructible historical vintage**, and a change of generation is not a verified revision: **Replay on currently available history; original vintages not guaranteed.** The **View summary** in the Query tab says all of this for a view in one place, for a screenshot: the place and level, each channel's measure, basis, unit, transform, policy, mapping id, cohort, fit cutoff, support and clipping (the rows of its Details popover, so the two cannot disagree), what the Rows are, the axes' domains, the profile tracks, the data, canonical and replay cutoffs, the model's status, the appearance and its version, and that original vintages are not recorded and that no hosted export or immutable snapshot is offered. It is built from those records and copies as text; nothing leaves the page unless it is copied.

Efficiency (and the diagonal chooser) carry a range-derived model: ISO_B = 0.486, so the expected ratio is 2^(ISO_B − 1) = 0.70; for the diagonal also ISO_A = −1.06, fitted on levels n = 6 to 13 of the history from 2021-01-01, extracted on 2026-09-24. The exact fit time, precision and method version are unknown and stay unknown. The status follows the effective cutoff in every mode: before 2026-09-24 00:00 UTC the model is **retrospective** (estimated on later data), within that day **timing unverified**, and from 2026-09-25 00:00 UTC eligible by the conservative bound. Outside levels 6 to 13 (both the level and its parent for Efficiency) the view says **Model extrapolated beyond fitted levels** and still draws equality to the declared model. The recorded page's cutoff lies within the extraction day, so it reads timing unverified. The status travels with a portable view even when its scale is eligible.

## Persistence and migration

Every address starts with `vis=2` and names its appearance (`ap=`); an unversioned nonempty payload is legacy, and a bare address is ambiguous and is treated as a new default with a one-time notice. A legacy import keeps its feature, period and line choices and explicitly migrates the hidden rate/rank colours to Amount, Value and Explore and Relative volume to version 2, naming the old and the new interpretation once per payload and tab; no claim is made to recover unsaved old colours. The limits are an 8,192-character address, 257 rank knots, a 1 MiB decoded portable payload and 16 active scales, enforced when reading and writing; over the address limit the address degrades to scale ids, then to a **settings-only URL, not exact calibration**, and the full portable code is always available. Imports are validated (versions, units, finite positive parameters, monotone knots, contexts, hashes, sizes), decompression is bounded, and nothing in a payload is executed; unknown versions are rejected with a reason. A scale descriptor is **not an immutable market-data snapshot**: new or revised history can change measurements while the mapping stays fixed. Inactive Explore-cache history does not travel with a view. Export or preserve your settings before the storage migration: old code does not understand v2 calibrations. Storage and history failures are shown, never silent, and the running view stays usable.

A scale a link or a code carries was fitted for one resolution context. A window refits its prices when it opens, so the level it settles at can differ from the level the scale was fitted at; the carried scale stays in the store for its own context, this view fits its own, and the page says so once, naming both levels (the notice "The scale in this link was fitted at n=…, m=…; this window shows n=…, m=…"), instead of drawing a different colour without saying why. Focus, Show all and Inspect are temporary and never enter an address, a code or the storage.

## Testing data

The tests use the recorded snapshot only for the measures it contains (cells, volumes, counts, taker-buy amounts); path, dwell, highs, lows and bars come from explicitly synthetic trade sequences with hand-computed or independent exact-rational expectations, and a fake cube validates the page's behaviour, not the real Origo service.

## Snapshot provenance and format

`data/snapshot.json` contains real recorded Binance BTCUSDT spot measures exported by the earlier Dyad prototype, not simulated trades. Its metadata records each block's coverage, exponents, source tile names and totals. All blocks share the snapshot's cutoff.

Each `gzip_base64` block decodes to MSC1:

- 32-byte header: magic `MSC1`, time exponent at byte 4, price exponent at byte 5, start/end column indices at bytes 8/12, record count at byte 16; integers are little-endian.
- Columnar arrays: Float64 volume, Float64 buy volume, UInt32 time column, UInt32 price row, UInt32 count, UInt32 buy count.

The seven-day block contains base cells. The 30-day archive and full-history overview contain coarser source cells. The application aggregates representable larger grids locally. Browser `DecompressionStream` support is required.

## Reading a cell without hue

A cell's value is its fill, and nothing about it depends on the hue alone. Where a signed cell is at least 12 CSS px across each way it carries a **sign mark** at its centre: a plus above the midpoint, a minus below it, a ring at it (the midpoint is a drawn fill, never the surface); the mark is the ink or the surface colour, whichever contrasts more with its fill, snapped to whole pixels so that what is rendered is the formula's contrast, and it covers at most a fifth of the smallest cell that carries it. Below that size the sign is in the readout, the Cells table and Inspect, and the footer says how many cells were left unmarked. A cell with no value is never a colour or a zero: undefined, pending, failed and unsupported cells have their own patterns in the neutral state ink, an unsigned zero is an outline and a balanced signed cell the midpoint with its ring; the several undefined cases that share one hatch are told apart by their words. A reference line is named by its tag, its pattern and, where it has one, its endpoint glyph, whatever its hue. `docs/color-matrix.md` screens every pair of these under normal vision, grayscale and the protan, deutan and tritan simulations at severities 0.5 and 1.0 (a model, not a person), and `docs/visual-contract.md` section 13 records the palette. **Inspect** (E) reads any position, including a cell with no trade, through the same readout record as the hover; on the lens it reads the finer record the lens draws, not the coarse cell under it.

## Historical evidence

For each displayed grid, the engine uses the loaded tiers that can represent it and, live, the cube's history of that level (each column's POC and volumes, up to the last 100,000 columns), over all price rows: the visible price window never conditions the historical population, so the same anchor gives the same base rates whichever band is on screen. The finest source owns overlapping columns. Tiles read for the view are never part of it, so where the view has been doesn't change the result. A state combines POC direction, buy-share third and volume third. Thresholds use columns before the anchor; candidate outcomes end no later than the anchor. Missing contiguous POCs interrupt a case.

Unconditional and conditional distributions use the same qualifying history. Cases, price bounds, source provenance and sample sizes are visible. Matching samples below 30 retain counts but suppress percentages and the matching boxes on the chart. These descriptive distributions are not probability calibration, independent trials, or validation of a trading strategy.

POC barriers are first crossings by column-end POCs over the selected horizon. Intracell trade ordering and true trade-price barrier first-touch cannot be recovered from this aggregated snapshot.

## The diagonal through the resolution lattice

Measured over the full history from 2021-01-01 (n = 6..13 on the 2026-09-24 extraction), the median price range of a column grows with its duration as `log2(range / 125) = -1.06 + 0.486 n`, an exponent of 0.49, close to a square-root law. The diagonal is the lattice path `m = round(-1.06 + 0.486 n)`, along which one column is about one row tall. Diagonal zoom scales the time window by a factor and the price window by its square root, and the auto level keeps the price exponent on that path. The plane marks the path so a deliberate step off it is visible.

The lattice the explorer can reach is `n = 0..20` (56.25 seconds to about 683 days per column) and `m = 0..9` (125 to 64,000 USDT per row); the plane shows all of it, and every stepper and clamp shares the same limits. In diagonal mode a time-only step (the bracket keys, the time steppers) keeps `m` on the path; an explicit level (a plane click, a price step, a pinned lens, an imported query) that changes the level to one off the path releases diagonal mode, and a step that clamps back to the current level changes nothing.

## Portable views

The page's address holds the view: `w` for a window (`15m`, `30m`, `1h`, `4h`, `12h`, `24h`, `7d`, `30d`, `1y`, `ytd`, `lastyear`, `all`), or `t` and `p` for a rectangle as UTC times and USDT prices; `r` for the level when it is locked; then each setting that differs from its default (`f`, `mode`, `pane`, `rows`, `period`, `level`, `marks`, `lines`, `sel`, `at`, `replay`, `tab`, `outcome`, `h`, `dist`). `mode` is one of `volume`, `flow`, `delta`, `cascade`, `trades`, `flowtrades`, `size`, `path`, `dwell` and `geometry` (`path` and `dwell` live only); `pane` is one of `volume`, `delta`, `trades`, `size`, `efficiency`, `choppiness`, `perpath`, `rsi1d`, `rsi4h` and `macd1d` (the last five live only), and is left out for Same as cells; `rows` is one of `volume`, `delta`, `relvol` and `time` (live only), and is left out while the underlay is off; `pc` is the profile tracks' comparison, `a` or `s`, left out for independent axes, and `po` is `1` where the folded track is open; `period` is one of the POC lines' periods (as `lines` lists them), `all`, or a day as `YYYY-MM-DD`, and is left out for 90 days; `level` is the level line's price in USDT, the centre of the price row it was placed on, and is left out without one; `marks` lists the overlays on, `poc`, `area` and `untested`, or `none` (an older address's `time`, the retired Time at price overlay, is ignored); `lines` lists the lines on: the POC lines, `1d`, `wk`, `7d`, `mo`, `30d`, `90d`, `yr`, `1y`, `3y` and days as `YYYY-MM-DD`; the volume profile's `va` (value areas), `dpoc` (daily POC and value area), `udpoc` and `uwpoc` (untested daily and weekly POCs, live only); the session levels, `dopen`, `pdhlc`, `wopen` and `mopen` (live only); structure, `ath`, `pch`, `swing4h`, `swing1d`, `fib30`, `fib90` and `fibswing` (live only); the moving averages, `ema21`, `sma50`, `sma100`, `sma200`, `gdcross`, `sma200w`, `bmsb`, `ema3m`, `ema15m`, `ema1h`, `ema4h`, `bb15m`, `bb1h`, `bb4h` and `bb1d` (live only); the VWAPs, `svwap`, `avwaph`, `avwapl` and days as `vwap:YYYY-MM-DD` (live only); and the clock, `cday`, `cweek`, `cmonth`, `funding`, `usopen`, `cme` and `deribit`. A window is relative to the cutoff; a rectangle is absolute. Unreadable parts fall back to their defaults, and a replay or selection outside the page's history is dropped.

The JSON query can be copied and reapplied, and an `origo-cube:` code also preserves selected visual settings and replay context; both are still accepted. Named views and the workspace are saved only in the local browser, and each tab's history only in that tab; no telemetry or external storage is used.

## Live cube blocks

Served by `tools/cube_bridge.py` on the Origo host, every block is a cube query at one level: `time_index` and `price_index` are the block's column and row, `volume`, `trade_count`, `taker_buy_volume` and `taker_buy_trade_count` its four measures, travelling as MSC2 (counts as 64-bit floats, exact below 2⁵³). The pack's tiers are read up to the cube's `data_cutoff` (the end of contiguous coverage, a minute edge), fixed once per pack, so the base column that holds it is open: it has the latest trades and gains more. The pack carries the cube's `data_cutoff` and `canonical_through` as well; cells after `canonical_through` come from provisional minutes that the day's archive later replaces, and the chart marks where they begin.

A rectangle's numbers are the cube's own: `/cube/query` reads the rectangle from the cube up to the last complete base column before the pack's cutoff and adds the open column from the pack's own base cells, then sums totals and POCs as the cube does (exact sums; the lower row wins a tie; a truncated winning row keeps its full row's centre). The same route serves tiles, the lens's finer cells and POC lines; `/cube/columns` serves the continuations' history. The underlay's rows are the POC lines' `/cube/query` reads at m = 0; its time at price reads the same period with `motion=1`, whose answer ends at the pack's last complete base column and carries the cube's dwell total. `/cube/touched?n&b0&b1` serves Efficiency: over base columns [b0, b1) on whole parent columns, each column's USDT and the 125 USDT rows its trades touched at level n, and each parent's at n + 1, the rows across both its columns counted once. The bridge reads the rectangle's cells at (n, 0) as `/cube/query` does, the open column from the page's pack, and answers the counts and sums only; the page asks for 512 columns at a time, up to the last whole parent before the data's edge. Each read is refused unless every partition it read is one the page's pack read, at the same revision and build id (a new generation of the same build, from a component attached to it, changes no cell); the page then takes the cube's new pack in place and asks again, rather than mixing two cube states. An open page asks for new packs on its own: it receives the columns each tier gained when every partition its pack read is unchanged and every column it keeps holds the same cells, and otherwise the whole pack. The same cells have the same trade counts, and volumes equal to within a relative 10⁻¹²: the cube sums a cell's volume in whatever order its read runs, so two reads of unchanged data can differ in the last bits, while new trades always change the counts. A read that fails leaves its numbers unmeasured, or its level unavailable, rather than silently coarser.

`/cube/bars?n&b0&b1&pack` serves bars at a grid timeframe (n = 0..20) over base positions [b0, b1), b0 on a bar's edge, finite `b1` (fractional for replay), and at most 4,096 bars: the bridge asks the cube for `open`, `high`, `low`, `close` and `base_volume` at rows of 125 × 2²⁰ USDT in its second query slot, and answers MSCB: the 32-byte header (m 20), then 64-bit float arrays open, high, low, close, volume, taker-buy volume, BTC volume and trades, then the column as a 32-bit integer, one bar per column with trades, in time order. Like path and dwell, bars end at the pack's last complete base column, or earlier where the cube's measures end, and the answer says where (`end`). The page reads each timeframe in chunks of 4,096 bars aligned to the history's start, the chunk holding the latest data again from its latest bar with each new pack and from the day's start once provisional minutes are replaced; in replay it reads the bar holding the edge up to the edge. The 8-hour bars are read over the whole history for the days, structure, the daily and weekly averages, the daily oscillators and the VWAPs' anchors; the 4-hour bars for their swings, averages, bands and RSI; the hourly ones for the CME gap and the hourly averages and bands; and after them, the VWAPs' timeframe over the view's UTC days and the 15- and 3.75-minute bars over the view's chunks and the one before. Each day's profile for the daily and untested POCs that no loaded tier holds is a `/cube/tile` at n = 9 and m = 0, up to 64 days at a time.

Path and dwell travel as MSC3: MSC2 with four more 64-bit float arrays after the trade counts, path length (USDT), dwell (seconds), and the cell's highest and lowest trade (NaN without trades), holding the cells without trades too. They are read only while a view shows them, in the cube service's second query slot and never ahead of the view's own reads: `/cube/motion?tier` reads one tier of the page's pack with them, whole once and then, with `from`, only its columns from where the page's end; `/cube/tile` and `/cube/query` with `motion=1` read a tile or a rectangle with them, the rectangle with the cube's path and dwell totals. Each answer ends at the pack's last complete base column, or earlier where the cube's own coverage of them ends, and says where (`end`). The columns a tier keeps across packs read the same partitions at the same revision and build, except provisional minutes the day's archive has since replaced, which the cube may measure differently: those the page reads again.

## Candles (PRD-0004 / P4-S1)

Candles is a price surface: one exact OHLC record per occupied time interval, no price-cell geometry or scalar calibration. High/low/open/close project continuously through the price transform. Close greater than open uses the positive blue arm and a hollow body; lower uses the negative clay arm and a filled body; equality uses midpoint ink with contrasting neutral casing. Body width is 65% of an interval. Below 2 CSS px it collapses to a 1 px wick; a one-price record retains a horizontal stroke. Numerical prices are never altered for these marks.

The main timeframe is the requested n; the lens uses clamp(n − depth, 0, 20), independent of loaded cell resolution. Price resolution does not determine candle geometry. Pin promotes the lens timeframe. Selection measures its separate cube rectangle and neither crops nor recomputes OHLC. Fit uses candle low/high. Rows, profile, references and Columns remain independently configured; Columns “Same as cells” displays Volume while retaining its setting.

Live reads end at floor(cutoff), excluding the forming base interval. Replay in Candles preserves the exact anchor, including sub-base timestamp boundaries; the endpoint is exclusive, so a trade at that timestamp is excluded. Partial bars show “So far” and their measured-through time. Empty traded support, unavailable coverage, pending, failed and recorded-only states remain distinct. The snapshot contains no OHLC and cannot supply Candles.

A visible range plus one interval each side takes priority over optional indicator history in the existing second query slot. Unused main/lens cell tiles are suppressed. A locked range wider than 4,096 bars requests a centered window and leaves uncovered edges pending. Numerical range storage uses LRU eviction at 64 ranges, 65,536 records or 16 MiB estimated record storage (192 bytes per record). A changed pack refreshes open/provisional support and discards stale answers. Neither theme nor price-row changes require candle reads.

`E.candles` is the added frozen encoding namespace: `record(bar,n,through)`, `project(record,X,Y,priceUnit,scratch?)`, `paint(ctx,record,X,Y,priceUnit,inks,scratch?)`, `span(n,a,b,edge,buffer?)`, `Cache()` and `LIMITS`. It supplies exact records, continuous geometry and directional marks rather than scalar values. Existing cell-scale preferences round-trip through addresses, codes and storage and apply again when returning to a cell mode.

## Authored trend lines

Manual two-point trend lines are user analysis, not market observations, measurements or family references. Their anchors are integer UTC milliseconds and USDT cents; their chosen RGB is exact object identity with no quantitative or directional meaning. Solid segments keep a hollow hexagon and a named route to exact coordinates/RGB, including when hues coincide. Cells, Candles and Lens use the same continuous time/price projection and shared persistent overlay planner; inventory distinguishes Hidden, Off screen and Held back. Draft previews do not change committed Inspect readings.

Drawings remain visible in replay with the disclosure **User drawings may include later analysis**; a visible segment beyond the cutoff also says so. This is not contemporaneous analysis or a market-data snapshot. Chart links and browser history carry no authored objects. Complete v3 codes and saved Views do; legacy/v2 imports retain current drawings, and replacement preserves the prior nonempty collection before it commits. [Drawing contracts](drawing-api.md) describe validation and protected storage; [operator gates](operator-drawings.md) separate finite engineering color samples from human recognition.

## Captured cell comparison (PRD-0006 / P6-S1)

Compare stores an owned measurement snapshot from the currently committed cell frame: its original level and nominal half-open time/price bounds, observed intervals and exposure, finite or typed unavailable metrics, source/pack, capture time, measured-through support and completeness. Main, Lens, Inspect and table actions share this adapter. Known-zero and movement-only cells can be captured; an absent map entry alone does not prove a zero. Candles and other non-cell surfaces do not offer these actions. Re-adding the same identity opens its first capture. Update capture on a matching currently resolved cell explicitly replaces it, keeping order and the pinned reference.

The comparison set is Volume, Trades, Mean trade size, Taker-buy volume share, Taker-buy trade share, Net taker volume, Path / price span, Dwell share and distance from a selected loaded period POC. Supporting movement diagnostics and original row/period context remain labeled detail, without context ranks or extra metrics. Values retain their canonical formula version and unit. Amount/Intensity governs volume, trades and signed net volume. Auto compares exact observed seconds × price width: equal area uses Amount, differing area uses Intensity. An explicit Amount choice on unequal area says Geometry differs. Short exposure remains a separate measurement cue.

A reference is the per-metric median of finite, compatible, replay-eligible values, or the independently eligible value of a pinned cell. It is never a pooled volume/trade ratio. Differences use value − reference; share differences are percentage points. Nonnegative amount/intensity/mean-size percentages require a positive reference; signed net volume has no percentage change. Competition ranks use exact stored numbers, larger first, with collection order preserving ties; POC distance ranks nearest first. Unavailable records remain in the collection and sort last. A sole eligible value says Only comparable cell. All off-page eligible records participate in statistics; pages mount at most 24 Cards or Matrix rows.

One POC choice freezes the price at a ready, nonempty period-profile row centre and its actual period, row resolution, approximation, measured cutoff and source/pack. It schedules no read. For price p and nominal band [low,high), distance is max(low−p,p−high,0). The lower edge is Contains and the excluded upper edge Touches; both have distance zero. Pending, failed and unloaded POCs are absent from the picker. Column/buy/value-area reference objects are outside this picker.

Capture retains a replay-limited frame as captured. Each metric and numeric supporting detail has separate actual aggregate supportEnd and snapshot knownThrough. In replay both must be established and at or before the effective edge; otherwise the whole stored metric is unavailable, with no numeric DOM attribute, difference, rank or copy payload. A clipped denominator does not establish a numerator's boundary. Captured Relative volume uses the later actual support end of its current rectangle and volume-reference period, including when Rows displays Time at price. Moving forward or leaving replay restores the same snapshot; it does not reconstruct the rest of a partial cell. Structural whole-cell knownAt and finality remain separate. This does not provide historical revision reconstruction.

The dedicated instrument-scoped sessionStorage record is comparison:v1:BTC/USDT, comparisonVersion 1, independent of visualVersion and the view codec. It owns collection, focus/reference, basis/sort/view/page, selected POC and expanded/restoration layout. Reload restores this tab; duplicated tabs subsequently diverge. Captures enter neither addresses, named Views nor chart history. Whole UTF-8 records have a 4 MiB read/write cap without an item-count cap or eviction. Oversized changes leave existing work unchanged; quota/access failure retains running work with Unsaved comparison. Rejected raw text is retained, unapplied; Retry cannot overwrite it. Confirmed Discard stored record permits saving the current valid work. Presentation saves are coalesced after visible updates, using cached immutable capture text; ordinary pointer/chart/replay navigation does not serialize collections.
